## 1. Akkadian

**Primary example: the stele of Hammurabi (Louvre Sb 8), law 21**

- **Form attested:** bi-tam (CDLI ATF `bi-tam`), syllabic spelling. Original script: cuneiform; no Unicode cuneiform is given in the CDLI record.
  - Context lines, CDLI ATF: `14. szum-ma a-wi-lum / 15. bi-tam / 16. ip-lu-usz` (šumma awīlum bītam ipluš).
  - The word is not bracketed or marked as damaged in the CDLI transliteration.
  - The normalization *bītam* (accusative) is my own parsing of the ATF, not text from the page.
  - The line gloss "if a man broke into a house" is also my own reading. CDLI's record has no translation, so check it against a published translation before putting it on a slide.
- **Meaning:** "house." Oracc glossary sense 2 of bītu is "house" (734 of 754 instances in RINAP).
- **Object:** quoted from the CDLI record P249253:
  - Artifact type: stela
  - Designation: "RIME 4.03.06.add21, ex. 01"
  - Museum no.: "Sb 00008", Louvre Museum, Paris, France
  - Period: "Old Babylonian (ca. 1900-1600 BC)"
  - Provenience: "Susa (mod. Shush)"
  - Date referenced: Hammurapi
  - Publications listed include "Codex Hammurapi" and "RIME 4".
- **Reference:** law 21, CDLI column 9, lines 15 (bi-tam) and 14–16 (clause).
  - These are CDLI's ATF line numbers. Column and line numbers in printed editions may differ.
- **Other syllabic spellings on the same stele** (from the CDLI ATF):
  - Prologue, col. 2 l. 66: `pa-qi2-id bi-tim`
  - Law 9, col. 7 l. 44: `a-na bi-it`
  - Law 16: `i-na bi-ti-szu`
  - Law 32: `szum-ma i-na bi-ti-szu`
  - The logogram É (`e2`) also occurs, mostly in temple names such as `e2-babbar` and `e2-sag-il2`.
- **URLs:**
  - CDLI record: https://cdli.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/artifacts/249253
  - CDLI photo (small): https://cdli.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/dl/photo/P249253.jpg
  - CDLI line art (large): https://cdli.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/dl/lineart/P249253_l.jpg
  - Reusable photo of the whole stele: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Code_of_Hammurabi-Sb_8-IMG_7755-white.jpg
- **Image rights:**
  - CDLI terms (https://cdli.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/terms-of-use), quoted: "Images presented online by the research project Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative (Oxford/Berlin/Los Angeles) are for the non-commercial use of students, scholars, and the public." Also: "Digitized images of photographs and original cuneiform text artifacts are subject to copyright laws and, except where noted otherwise, are the property of the institutions that own the artifacts." **Not downloaded.**
  - Wikimedia Commons photo: "CC BY-SA 3.0 fr", author "Rama", "Own work", attribution required. **Downloaded** to `images/akkadian_hammurabi_stele_Sb8_Rama_CC-BY-SA-3.0-fr.jpg` (1920 px), with a `.txt` attribution note beside it. It shows the whole stele, not a close-up of law 21.
- **Confidence: high.** bi-tam is a clear syllabic writing of bītu in an unrestored line of a famous, well-documented object.
  - Remaining uncertainty: column and line numbering differ between editions.

**Glossary entry (lexicon source, labeled as such): Oracc RINAP glossary, bītu [HOUSE] (N)**

- URL: https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/rinap/cbd/akk/B.html (entry id `rinap/akk/bītu[house]`)
- Quoted from the page:
  - "754 instances"
  - Written forms: "É; GIŠ.É; É.KI.NÁ.MEŠ [bīt mayyālī]; É.MEŠ; É.MEŠ-šu-nu; É.MUN [bīt ṭabti]; É-su; É-šu; É-šú; É-ti-šú-nu; LÚ.KU₄.É [ērib bīti]"
  - Senses: "1. container (1x/0%) 2. house (734x/97%) 3. room (1x/0%) 4. temple (18x/2%)"
- RINAP license, from the project's text pages: "The annotated edition is released under the Creative Commons Attribution Share-Alike license 3.0."

Its note 18 reads: "Borger (Asarh. p. 86), the CAD (T p. 70 sub takbitu), and others emend URU É-ti-šú-[nu] to URU <tak>-pit-šú-[nu]". That reading is disputed, so **do not use it.**

## 2. Ugaritic

**Primary example: Kirta epic, KTU 1.14, col. iii line 38 (Musée National d'Alep)**

- **Form attested:** bbty, i.e. *b-bt-y* "in my house". EUPT transliteration: `iii 38 pd . in . bbty . ttn`.
  - EUPT vocalization: *pa dā ˀêna bi bêtiya tâtin*.
  - No restoration or damage marks on this line in the EUPT transliteration.
  - Original script: Ugaritic alphabetic cuneiform. EUPT shows the tablet as an autograph drawing, not as Unicode. For a slide, bt in Unicode Ugaritic is 𐎁𐎚 (U+10381, U+1039A); I supplied that rendering, it is not from the page.
- **Meaning:** "house." EUPT's German translation of iii 38: "Vielmehr gib, was in meinem Haus fehlt" ("Rather, give what is lacking in my house"; the English is mine).
- **Object:** quoted from EUPT's metadata panel:
  - Schriftträger: "Tafel (Ton)" (clay tablet)
  - Fundort: "Ugarit / Ras Shamra, Acropolis, House of the High Priest"
  - Maße: "220 mm (H) x 170 mm (B) x 30 mm (T)"
  - Schreiber (scribe): "ˀIlimilku"
  - Genre: "Epos. Erste Tafel des Kirtu-Epos." (first tablet of the Kirta epic)
  - Grabungsnummer (excavation no.): "RS 2.[003] + 3.324 + 3.344 + 3.414"
  - Museumsnummer: "M 8218 = A 2750 (AO 17.190) (Musée National d'Alep)"
  - Publikationsnummer: "KTU1–3 1.14 = CTA 14 = UDB 1.14"
  - EUPT gives no absolute date on this panel. (Ugarit texts are conventionally Late Bronze Age, but that is not from the page.)
- **Other attestations on the same tablet** (EUPT transliteration → EUPT translation):
  - ii 43 `yḥd . bth . sgr` → *yaḥīdu bêtahu sagara*, "Der einzig Verbliebene schließe sein Haus"
  - iv 21 `aḥd . bth . y⸢s⸣gr` (one sign marked damaged, not in bt)
  - iv 40 `hm . ḥry . bty` → "Wenn ich Ḥurriya in mein Haus nehmen kann"
  - ii 29 / iv 10 `lbt . ḫbr` → place name "Bêtu-Ḫabūri"
  - i 7–8 `bt / [m]⸢l⸣k`, "[des Kö]nigs Haus". The king's name is partly restored here; avoid it, or flag it on the slide.
- **URLs:**
  - Edition: https://eupt.uni-goettingen.de/edition.html (opens on KTU 1.14)
  - Downloadable autograph copy: https://eupt.uni-goettingen.de/api/eupt/img/KTU_1.14_Download.webp
  - Photos: EUPT lists WSRP/InscriptiFact photo IDs. For iii 31–41, for example, "UC15302932" and "UC15303974". Those photos are on InscriptiFact, which requires registration; I did not access them.
- **Rights, quoted from EUPT:**
  - "Lizenz: CC-BY-SA-4.0"
  - "Bildlizenz: CC-BY-SA-4.0"
  - Bildnotizen: "Die Kopie wurde von Noah Kröll erstellt (2025-04-03)." (The copy was made by Noah Kröll.)
  - Citation line given: "KTU 1.14. Edition des ugaritischen poetischen Textkorpus (EUPT). Version Draft 3.2. https://eupt.uni-goettingen.de/ [2025-07-18]. Bearbeitet von Clemens Steinberger, unter Mitarbeit von Noah Kröll, Christiane Meinhold, Reinhard Müller, Marion Possiel und Frilla Smilgies."
  - **Downloaded** the autograph drawing to `images/ugaritic_KTU_1.14_EUPT_autograph_CC-BY-SA-4.0.webp`, with an attribution `.txt`. It is a drawing of the tablet, not a photograph.
- **Confidence: high.** The line is unrestored in a current scholarly edition, and the edition itself translates the word as Haus.
  - Caveat: EUPT marks this as "Version Draft 3.2".

## 3. Phoenician

**Primary example: sarcophagus of Eshmunazar II, king of Sidon (Louvre AO 4806), inscription line 18**

- **Form attested:** bt, Phoenician script 𐤁𐤕, twice in line 18:
  - Phoenician script: `𐤁𐤕 𐤋𐤁𐤏𐤋 𐤑𐤃𐤍 𐤅𐤁𐤕 𐤋𐤏𐤔𐤕𐤓𐤕`
  - Transliteration: `bt lbʿl ṣdn wbt lʿštrt`
  - Vocalization: *bīt labaʿl ṣīdōn webīt laʿaštart*
  - Neither instance is bracketed in the edition.
  - Line 17 has `bnn bt lʾšmn` (*banīnū bīt laʾèšmūn*), also unbracketed, and plural `btm` (*bītīm*).
  - **Caution:** line 16's `[bt ʿštr]t` is a restoration.
  - **Caution:** line 15's `bt mlk` is *bat* "daughter," a different word. Don't pick that one.
- **Meaning:** "temple" (house of a god). Translation on the page: "a temple for Baal of Sidon, and a temple for Ashtart, the Name of Baal."
- **Object:** quoted from the Louvre record (https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010120357):
  - Title: "Sarcophage d'Eshmunazor"
  - Inventory: "Numéro principal : AO 4806"; catalogue numbers include "KAI 14", "CIS I 2", "CIS I 3"
  - Date: "Achéménide (Premier quart du Ve siècle avant J.-C.) et Achéménide (bibliographie/travail de référence) (-500 - -475 et vers -525)"
  - Material: "basalte (Amphibolite noire = basalte des carrières de la vallée de Hammamat, Égypte.)"
  - Dimensions: "Hauteur : 256 cm"
  - Place of discovery: "Saïda Magharat Tabloun = Saïda nécropole", found 1855
  - Location: "Sully, [AO] Salle 311"
  - Script and language: "phénicien"
- **Reference:** lid inscription, line 18 (also line 17). The Louvre gives the French translation by line, credited "Trad. P. Bordreuil, E. Gubel, Art Phénicien, 2003, n° 94, pp. 101-102". Line 18: "pour le(s) dieu(x) des Sidoniens à Sidon côtière, le temple pour le Ba'al de Sidon et le temple pour Ashtart-nom-de-Ba'al…" The museum's line numbers match the Wikipedia table.
- **Source type:**
  - The Phoenician text (script, transliteration, vocalization) comes from the English Wikipedia article "Sarcophagus of Eshmunazar II" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus_of_Eshmunazar_II). Its line-by-line table cites Gzella 2011 and Amadasi Guzzo 2019.
  - The Louvre record gives only a translation, not the Phoenician text.
  - For a primary edition, the Louvre bibliography lists KAI 14 (Donner–Röllig 1966) and CIS I 3 (with a public archive.org link: http://archive.org/details/CorpusInscriptionumSemiticarumI1).
- **Louvre images:**
  - The record has 16 photos with a "Download image" button. Caption on the first photo: "© 2019 Musée du Louvre, Dist. GrandPalaisRmn / Raphaël Chipault".
  - I did not find a reuse license on the record page itself, so I did **not** download the Louvre photos.
  - JSON record: https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010120357.json
- **Image:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eshmunazar_II_sarcophagus_(cleaned_up).jpg
- **Image rights, quoted from Commons:**
  - "CC BY-SA 4.0"
  - Artist: "Onceinawhile"
  - Credit: "This image has been extracted from another file"
  - Attribution required.
  - **Downloaded** to `images/phoenician_eshmunazar_II_sarcophagus_AO4806_Onceinawhile_CC-BY-SA-4.0.jpg` with an attribution `.txt`. It shows the whole sarcophagus; the lid inscription is legible only at full resolution.
- **Confidence: high** for the object and line, since the Louvre record and its published translation agree on line 18 "le temple pour le Ba'al de Sidon".
  - **Medium-high** for the exact Phoenician letter-by-letter text, which comes from Wikipedia's cited table. Check it against KAI 14 or CIS before putting the script on a slide.

## 4. Aramaic / Syriac

**Primary example (Old Aramaic): the Tel Dan stele, `bytdwd` "House of David"**

- **Form attested:** `bytdwd`, written as one word with no word divider. CAL cites it as "TDanStel 9 ml]k | bytdwd : King of the Davidic dynasty."
  - The *ml]k* before it is partly restored in CAL's citation. `bytdwd` itself is not bracketed.
  - Original script: Old Aramaic in the Phoenician-type alphabet. 𐤁𐤉𐤕𐤃𐤅𐤃 is my own Unicode rendering for a slide, not from a page.
- **Meaning:** "house" in the sense of dynasty. CAL places this citation under the sub-sense "in construct with (or implying) a proper noun: dynastic house or the like."
  - **Caution:** a web-search summary of secondary sources noted that "some scholars dispute the reading because the words are written together without a word divider." I did not verify that from a primary source. Present it as the majority reading.
- **Object:** quoted from the Jewish Museum (New York) wall text, https://thejewishmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/JM_Collection_Tel_Dan_Section_Text_FINAL-2.pdf:
  - "Written by an Aramaic king in the ninth century BCE, the text on this fragmentary basalt slab references the 'House of David'"
  - "The slab is composed of three fragments discovered in Tel Dan in northern Israel. The first was found embedded in an ancient stone wall in 1993, while the other two were unearthed in 1994"
  - "Loaned by The Israel Museum, Jerusalem, from the collection of the Israel Antiquities Authority"
  - "The Israel Museum, Jerusalem, where the Tel Dan Stele is on permanent display"
- **Holding / number:** Israel Museum, Jerusalem (IAA collection).
  - A web-search summary reported the IAA accession numbers "1996-125 and 1993-3162" from a Jewish Museum caption, but they are **not** in the PDF I extracted. I could not find an Israel Museum (imj.org.il) object page. **Treat the numbers as unverified.**
- **Reference:** line 9 per CAL's siglum "TDanStel 9".
- **URLs:**
  - CAL entry: https://cal.huc.edu/oneentry.php?lemma=by%20N&cits=all (search lexicon; see sense 1(1), the dynastic-house sub-sense)
  - Image: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aramaic_Inscription_on_Basalt_Monument,_Dan,_9th_Century_BC_(43167226572).jpg
  - Alternative image: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:JRSLM_300116_Tel_Dan_Stele_01.jpg ("CC BY-SA 4.0", "Oren Rozen", "The Tel Dan Stele on display at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem")
- **Image rights, quoted from Commons:**
  - "CC0", artist "Gary Todd from Xinzheng, China", description "Israel Museum, Jerusalem, Israel."
  - **Downloaded** to `images/aramaic_tel_dan_stele_GaryTodd_CC0.jpg` with a `.txt` note.
- **Confidence: high** for the word and the object. **Low** for the IAA numbers, which I could not verify.

**Lexicon entry (labeled as such): CAL, "by, bytˀ (bay, baytā) n.m./f. house; structure; place"**

- URL: https://cal.huc.edu/oneentry.php?lemma=by%20N&cits=all
- Sense 1: "house: a building for dwelling", Common Aramaic. Citations on the page:
  - "TAD B2.1 R.3 יהבת לי תרע ביתא זילך למבנה אגר 1 תמה : you have granted me your house-gate to build a single wall there" (an Elephantine papyrus)
  - "Dan 6:11 עַל לְבַיְתֵהּ … : he went into his house with windows opened for him in the clearstory facing Jerusalem"
  - "TgO Gen19:2 זוּרוּ כְעַן לְבֵית עַבדְכוֹן וְבִיתוּ : please come over to your servant's house and spend the night"
- Other senses on the page: "household", "family", "dynastic house", "school", "with divinity : temple", "with royalty : palace", "strophe; section of a literary work" (Syr), and "(astron.) house, astrological sign" (Syr, Man, LJLA).
  - The Syriac "strophe" sense parallels the Arabic/Persian *bayt* "verse," which is a nice bridge in the talk.
- **Syriac (Peshitta) citations on the page:**
  - "P Gn7:1 ܥܘܠ ܐܢܬ ܘܟܠܗ ܒܝܬܟ ܠܩܒܘܬܐ : you and your whole family enter the ark"
  - "P Jn14:2 ܣܲܓܼ݁ܝܐܝܼܢ̈ ܐܸܢܘ݂ܢ ܐܲܘܵܢܸܐ̈ ܒܸ݁ܝܬ݂ ܐܵܒ݂ܝ : many are the rooms in my Father's house"
  - "P Acts10:2 … ܗܘ݂ ܘܒ݂ܲܝܬܸ݁ܗ ܟܽܠܸܗ : he and his whole household were god-fearing"
  - Syriac form: ܒܝܬܐ / ܒܝܬ (*baytā / bēṯ*).
- **Rights:** the page footer reads "© The Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon ©". The site's front page also asks: "Please do not try to scrape our site." I looked up the entry by hand in the browser only.

## 5. Hebrew

**Primary example: Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaᵃ), column 46, Isaiah 56:7**

- **Form attested:** in the Masoretic text, from Sefaria's "Miqra according to the Masorah", CC-BY-SA:
  - בְּבֵ֣ית תְּפִלָּתִ֔י (*bə-bêt təpillātî*)
  - בֵיתִ֔י בֵּית־תְּפִלָּ֥ה (*bêtî bêt-təpillâ*)
  - **Caution:** this is the Masoretic wording. The Israel Museum's scroll viewer reports that 1QIsaᵃ has "well over 2,600" textual variants and does not give the scroll's own spelling of this verse. I did not find a transcription of the scroll's wording, or the line number within column 46. Check the scroll's reading (e.g. in the Ulrich & Flint edition; the viewer page credits their translation) before quoting it letter for letter.
- **Meaning:** "house," here God's house (the Temple). JPS (Sefaria, CC-BY-NC): "And let them rejoice in My house of prayer… For My House shall be called / A house of prayer for all peoples."
- **Object:** quoted from the Israel Museum, Digital Dead Sea Scrolls, http://dss.collections.imj.org.il/isaiah:
  - "The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsa a) is one of the original seven Dead Sea Scrolls discovered in Qumran in 1947. It is the largest (734 cm) and best preserved of all the biblical scrolls… The 54 columns contain all 66 chapters… Dating from ca. 125 BCE"
  - Holding: The Israel Museum, Jerusalem (Shrine of the Book). The page gives no inventory number other than the siglum 1QIsaᵃ.
- **Reference:** column 46. The museum's viewer data file (http://dss.collections.imj.org.il/viewer/data/isaiah.json) lists `"id": 46 … "range": "55:8-57:2"`, which contains 56:7.
- **URLs:**
  - Viewer: http://dss.collections.imj.org.il/isaiah (works over http only; https failed)
  - Museum column image: http://tiles.imj.org.il/columns/isaiah/isaiah46.jpg
  - Public-domain full scroll: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Great_Isaiah_Scroll_MS_A_(1QIsa)_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg
- **Rights:**
  - Israel Museum site footer: "Copyright © The Israel Museum, Jerusalem". No reuse license, so the museum image was **not** saved to images/.
  - Wikimedia Commons file: "Public domain", credit "NAEMzlf5AD5yOQ at Google Cultural Institute maximum zoom level".
  - I cropped column 46 from that file and **saved** it as `images/hebrew_1QIsaa_col46_Isa55-8_57-2_GoogleArtProject_PD.jpg` (1180 × 2506 px), with a `.txt` note. I checked the crop visually against the museum's own column 46 image: same opening line, same stain.
- **Confidence: high** that 56:7 is on this column.
  - **Medium** for the exact spelling shown on the slide, since it is the Masoretic text and the scroll may differ.
  - **Not verified:** which line of the column holds the verse.

## 6. Ancient North Arabian

**Primary example: Safaitic inscription LP 949, al-ʿĪsāwī (Syria): `l bnkf h- bt` "This house [belongs] to Bnkf"**

- **Form attested:** `bt`, with the definite article: `h- bt`. OCIANA transliteration: "l bnkf h- bt".
  - No brackets or uncertainty marks in the OCIANA text.
  - Original script: Safaitic (an Ancient North Arabian script). OCIANA's image is a hand copy, with the letters running right to left.
- **Meaning:** "house". OCIANA's translation: "This house [belongs] to Bnkf". The bracketed "[belongs]" is OCIANA's supplied verb, not a restoration of text.
- **Object:** quoted from the OCIANA record:
  - Siglum: "LP 949"; Language and Script: "Safaitic"
  - Site: "Al-ʿĪsāwī, Rif Dimašq Governorate, Syria"; Date Found: "1904–1905"; Current Location: "In situ"
  - Subject: "Genealogy"; Old OCIANA ID: "#0009533"
  - Reference: Littmann, E. *Safaïtic Inscriptions*. Princeton University Archaeological Expeditions to Syria in 1904–1905 and 1909, Div. IV, Sect. C. Leiden: Brill, 1943.
  - The rock is still in situ, so there is no museum number.
  - **No date is given.** (Safaitic texts are generally dated to roughly the last centuries BCE through the first centuries CE, but that is not from this record.)
